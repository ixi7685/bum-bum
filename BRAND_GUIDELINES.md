# Brand Guidelines — Locked & Non-Negotiable

> This document exists to protect trust.
>
> Growth pressure, content pressure, and user demand will constantly push the platform toward shortcuts that feel helpful but quietly destroy credibility.
> This document defines the hard boundaries we do not cross, even if doing so would increase traffic, engagement, or revenue.
>
> **If something violates this document, it is wrong by definition.**

---

## 1. Failure Outcomes We Guard Against

These are the real risks we must actively prevent:

1. **Too abstract at first contact** — "Employment risk" can feel vague if not grounded fast.
2. **Accidental HR / institutional tone** — Even correct language can trigger distrust if it feels formal.
3. **"Sounds smart, but what do I actually get?"** — Users need clarity on use, not just concept.
4. **Perceived similarity to ratings sites** — Any ambiguity here kills differentiation.
5. **Overclaiming certainty** — Must stay analytical, not authoritative.

---

## 2. Adaptive Positioning Statement — LOCKED

> **This platform helps people understand employment risk in the context of their own situation.**
>
> Instead of generic company ratings, we analyze recurring patterns in how companies operate and adapt the results to your career stage, risk tolerance, constraints, and values.
>
> The same company can be stable for one person and risky for another. We make that difference clear before you commit.

⚠️ **This paragraph is the source of truth.**
Every page, post, pitch, and feature must align with it.

### Why This Survives the Stress Test

| Phrase | Why it works |
|--------|-------------|
| "In the context of their own situation" | Immediately grounds the idea. Reduces abstraction. Signals personalization without buzzwords. |
| "Recurring patterns" | Anchors credibility. Avoids opinions, reviews, or secrets. |
| "Adapt the results" | Reinforces that output changes with the user. Differentiates from static scores. |
| "Make that difference clear" | Plain language. No hype, no drama. Actionable without commanding. |
| "Before you commit" | Maps directly to the anxiety moment. Protective, not paternalistic. |

### Internal Lock Rule

> We are an intelligence layer, not a decision-maker.
> Our job is to surface how company patterns interact with different people, not to tell anyone what to do.
>
> **If something violates this, it violates Phase 0.**

## 3. ICP: Entry Wedge vs Universal Scope

### Entry Wedge (How we enter the market)

**Gen Z job seekers (18–30)**

- Early career, first real jobs
- High downside risk, low information power
- Digital-native, already distrust employer narratives
- Actively searching online before decisions

They are the fastest adopters, the loudest sharers, the lowest friction entry point.

### Universal Scope (Who this is actually for)

**Anyone making an employment decision where risk matters.**

The platform is age-agnostic, role-agnostic, industry-agnostic.
The questionnaire, not demographics, defines relevance.

> **Key internal rule:** Gen Z is the door. Personalization is the engine.

## 4. WHAT WE ARE NOT

### We are NOT a review site

We do not collect, surface, or prioritize individual opinions.
Single experiences are not evidence of patterns.

If a claim cannot survive aggregation, time, and cross-source validation, it does not belong here.

### We are NOT a rating platform

We do not assign universal scores, stars, rankings, or leaderboards.

There is no such thing as a "good company" or "bad company" in isolation.
Only patterns interacting with specific people in specific contexts.

### We are NOT an HR tool

We do not help companies hire, brand themselves, or improve employer perception.

Our obligation is to the decision-maker, not the employer.

Any feature that optimizes for employer comfort over user clarity is misaligned.

### We are NOT a "company exposure" or drama platform

We do not exist to shame, expose, or create outrage cycles.

We analyze behavior patterns, not scandals.
We surface risk, not controversy.

If something feels designed to provoke emotion rather than inform judgment, it does not belong.

### We are NOT a guarantee of outcomes

We do not promise safety, success, happiness, or career growth.

We provide information and context, not certainty.

Employment outcomes are probabilistic. Pretending otherwise is dishonest.

---

## 5. WHAT WE DO NOT DO

- **We do NOT publish single-review opinions.** One person's experience is not a pattern. We explicitly reject anecdotal conclusions, isolated testimonials, and emotionally compelling but statistically weak claims.

- **We do NOT score companies universally.** There are no global scores, "best places to work," or "avoid at all costs" labels. Any signal must be contextual, weighted by the user profile, and explainable.

- **We do NOT sell fear.** We do not use alarmist language, countdown framing, or worst-case emphasis to drive conversion. Fear produces clicks. Fear destroys trust. If a user feels pressured rather than informed, we failed.

- **We do NOT optimize for virality over accuracy.** We do not simplify, exaggerate, or dramatize findings to improve shareability. Accuracy scales slower than hype. We accept that tradeoff.

- **We do NOT pretend to have certainty where there isn't any.** Uncertainty is not a weakness. Hiding uncertainty is. If the data is thin, conflicted, or inconclusive, we say so explicitly.

## 6. CORE TONE PRINCIPLES

These rules apply everywhere: landing pages, TikTok scripts, UI microcopy, emails, reports, internal GTM drafts.

Language is not cosmetic. It signals intent, power, and bias.
If our language feels corporate, dramatic, or performative, trust collapses immediately.

### Human, not corporate

We write the way a thoughtful person explains something to another person.

- Avoid: HR phrasing, institutional language, motivational tone
- Prefer: plain words, direct sentences, everyday language
- **Test:** Would this sound normal if said out loud to a friend?

### Clear, not clever

Clarity beats creativity.

- Do not hide meaning behind metaphors
- Do not use clever phrasing that sacrifices precision
- Do not optimize for wit or memorability
- **Test:** Would two smart people interpret this the same way?

### Calm, not alarmist

We deal with risk, not panic.

- Do not escalate urgency artificially
- Do not use fear-based framing
- Do not imply imminent danger
- Prefer: neutral descriptions, proportional language, steady tone
- **Test:** Does this inform, or does it pressure?

### Honest about uncertainty

Uncertainty is part of the product.

- Explicitly acknowledge incomplete data, mixed signals, changing conditions
- Do not overstate confidence, hide ambiguity, or imply foresight we don't have
- **Test:** Are we being precise about what we know vs what we don't?

## 7. LANGUAGE RULES

### Words & Phrases to Avoid

Avoid language that signals authority over the user, hidden truth, moral judgment, or hype:

- "expose"
- "truth"
- "guarantee"
- "best / worst"
- "toxic" (without clear definition)
- "red flag" (without context)
- "must"
- "always / never"

If a word simplifies complexity or dramatizes uncertainty, don't use it.

### Language We Prefer

Language that describes patterns, explains tradeoffs, preserves agency:

- "tends to"
- "has shown a pattern of"
- "varies by role and timing"
- "more stable for"
- "higher risk for"
- "based on available data"

This keeps the tone analytical, not judgmental.

### Platform-Specific Note

Being on TikTok does not change these rules.
Short-form does not mean dramatic. Simple does not mean exaggerated.
We compress information, not truth.

## 8. ALLOWED MESSAGES (Use These Freely)

### ✅ Allowed Message 1
> "This company isn't risky in general. It may be risky for you."

### ✅ Allowed Message 2
> "We don't tell you where to work. We show you what usually happens."

### ✅ Allowed Message 3
> "Same company. Different outcomes. Depends on who you are."

These reinforce personalization, non-judgment, and decision support without authority.

---

## 9. FORBIDDEN MESSAGES (Never Use)

### ❌ Forbidden Message 1
> "Avoid this company"

*Why: Absolute, paternalistic, fear-based*

### ❌ Forbidden Message 2
> "Top companies / Worst companies"

*Why: Generic, misleading, Glassdoor energy*

### ❌ Forbidden Message 3
> "We expose the truth employers hide"

*Why: Conspiratorial, hype-driven, trust-destroying*

> **Litmus test:** If a sentence would get applause on LinkedIn, it's probably forbidden.

---

## 10. MANDATORY CHECKS

### Universal Copy Check

Before anything ships, ask:

1. Does this sound like HR, PR, or marketing?
2. Does this imply certainty where none exists?
3. Does this push the user emotionally instead of informing them?
4. Does this remove the user's agency?

If any answer is yes, rewrite.

### Internal Trust Check

Before shipping any feature, message, report format, content piece, or partnership, ask:

> **Does this feel closer to Glassdoor, LinkedIn, or corporate PR than to a neutral intelligence tool?**

If the answer is yes, it is wrong. No exceptions.

---

## 11. ENFORCEMENT RULES

### Trust is the product.

Once users believe we are ranking, judging, selling outrage, or taking sides — the platform collapses into noise.

These rules override:

- Growth goals
- Marketing ideas
- Partnership opportunities
- Monetization experiments
- Content ideas
- Conversion tactics

If something performs well but violates these rules, it still fails.
If something violates WHAT WE ARE NOT, it does not ship.

---

## 12. WHY THIS IS NON-NEGOTIABLE

The fastest way to destroy trust is:

- sounding smarter than you are
- sounding more certain than the data
- sounding like you want something from the user

Our language must always signal:

> **"We are here to help you think, not tell you what to do."**
